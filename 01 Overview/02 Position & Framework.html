Position & Framework: Design Technology Sequence

Purpose

Design Technology I and Design Technology II provide the sophomore-level technological foundation for contemporary graphic design practice.

While software skills remain important, the sequence positions technology not as a collection of tools, but as a design medium through which information is structured, translated, produced, and experienced across media.

Students investigate how technologies — including materials, standards, tools, workflows, systems, and emerging computational processes — shape design decisions and influence communication.

The sequence prepares students to work across print, screen, interaction, and emerging technologies while developing durable habits of systems thinking, production literacy, research, creative experimentation, and professional workflow.

Position Statement

Design Technology is the study of how information is structured, translated, produced, and made responsive across media.

Contemporary designers work within technological systems regardless of specialization. Whether designing publications, interfaces, products, environments, motion graphics, or emerging media experiences, designers must understand how information behaves across platforms, formats, audiences, and contexts.

Technology is understood broadly to include:

  • material systems
  • production systems
  • information systems
  • communication systems
  • computational systems

Examples include:

  • paper and print technologies
  • typography and grid systems
  • color and reproduction systems
  • file and workflow systems
  • web standards
  • component libraries
  • design systems
  • AI-assisted workflows

Students learn that technologies are not neutral. Every technology contains assumptions, defaults, possibilities, and constraints that influence design outcomes.

The purpose of Design Technology is therefore not software mastery for its own sake. The purpose is to develop designers who can:

  • understand media and production constraints
  • translate information across formats and contexts
  • build structured and scalable systems
  • design interactive and responsive experiences
  • critically engage emerging technologies
  • adapt to future tools and workflows

Technology becomes a vehicle for understanding design rather than an end in itself.

Rather than existing separately from the departmental framework, Design Technology provides a practical environment through which each pillar becomes visible and actionable.

Cognitive Framework

Design Technology investigates how information is structured, organized, translated, produced, and experienced across media.

Students learn to analyze:

  • structure
  • hierarchy
  • systems
  • interaction
  • behavior
  • production

through the lens of contemporary technology.

Technology is understood not simply as software, but as the environment through which design is created, distributed, reproduced, and experienced.

Sequence Philosophy

The sophomore sequence progresses from understanding media and production toward understanding systems and behavior.

Students move from creating individual outputs toward designing adaptable systems capable of operating across platforms, audiences, and contexts.

Design Technology I Design Technology II
Media Systems
Translation Interaction

Assets Components

This progression establishes a foundation for advanced coursework in interaction design, motion design, emerging media, design systems, professional practice, and future technologies.

Sophomore Design Technology Lens

Across both semesters, students repeatedly engage five core questions:

Question Focus
Structure How is information organized?
Translation How does information change across media?
System What rules create consistency and scalability?
Behavior How does information respond to users, context, or time?
Defaults What assumptions are built into the technology?

These questions provide continuity across projects, tools, and technological environments.

The concept of defaults encourages students to recognize that every technology contains embedded decisions.

Examples may include:

  • paper sizes
  • browser defaults
  • operating-system conventions
  • typographic conventions
  • templates
  • accessibility settings
  • AI-generated outputs

Students learn to evaluate, challenge, and intentionally work with these defaults.

Design Technology I

Media + Translation + Output

Course Description

Design Technology I introduces students to the technological foundations of contemporary design practice.

Students develop fluency with digital media, production workflows, structured information, and design translation across print, screen, and web environments.

The course emphasizes media literacy, organization, workflow, production, and material understanding while introducing systems thinking through typography, layout, publication design, and structured web media.

Core Questions

  • How does design change across media?
  • How do production constraints influence design decisions?
  • How is information structured and organized?
  • How do workflows support effective design practice?
  • How do technologies shape communication?
  • What assumptions are built into the tools and systems we use?

Student Outcome

Students become capable of creating, organizing, translating, and producing design across multiple media while understanding the systems that support production.

Design Technology II

Systems + Interaction + Behavior

Course Description

Design Technology II expands foundational technological literacy into systems thinking, interaction, responsiveness, and behavior.

Students move beyond individual artifacts toward designing adaptable systems that respond to users, content, context, and time.

The course emphasizes interaction design, design systems, prototyping, motion, responsive environments, and emerging technologies.

Core Questions

  • How do people interact with information?
  • How do systems create consistency and adaptability?
  • How does design behave over time?
  • How do technologies shape user experience?
  • How can systems evolve across contexts?
  • How do emerging technologies influence design practice?

Student Outcome

Students become capable of designing interactive, responsive, and scalable systems that support contemporary communication across media.

Semester Structure

Design Technology I

Unit 1 — Digital Foundations

Focus

Understanding the digital environment as a design environment.

Topics

  • operating systems
  • file organization
  • metadata
  • cloud and local storage
  • asset management
  • fonts and licensing
  • file formats
  • raster and vector media
  • workflow fundamentals
  • information organization
  • naming and versioning systems

Project Direction

Create and manage a personal design asset system.

Unit 2 — Media, Materials, and Translation

Focus

Understanding how visual information changes across media.

Topics

  • image editing
  • vector construction
  • asset preparation
  • format conversion
  • color systems
  • color spaces
  • reproduction technologies
  • paper systems
  • substrates
  • ink and print technologies
  • fidelity and reproduction
  • media constraints
  • adaptation across formats

Project Direction

Translate a visual concept across multiple media outputs while evaluating how material and technological constraints affect communication.

Unit 3 — Publication and Information Systems

Focus

Structure, hierarchy, and communication through layout systems.

Topics

  • typographic systems
  • hierarchy systems
  • modular scales
  • grids
  • information architecture
  • content organization
  • content hierarchy
  • publication workflows
  • print production

Project Direction

Design a publication system that organizes complex information across multiple pages and formats.

Unit 4 — Structured Screen Media

Focus

Design as structure rather than appearance.

Topics

  • HTML
  • CSS
  • semantic structure
  • accessibility
  • responsive layouts
  • content-first design
  • web standards
  • screen-based production
  • responsive typography

Project Direction

Translate a publication system into a responsive screen-based experience.

Design Technology II

Unit 1 — Interaction Foundations

Focus

Designing information for use and navigation.

Topics

  • information architecture
  • user journeys
  • user research
  • task analysis
  • wireframes
  • prototyping
  • usability evaluation

Project Direction

Design an interactive information experience.

Unit 2 — Components and Design Systems

Focus

Moving from assets toward reusable systems.

Topics

  • assets versus components
  • reusable patterns
  • components
  • variables
  • design tokens
  • documentation
  • governance
  • scalable systems

Project Direction

Build a component-based design system.

Unit 3 — Motion and Behavior

Focus

Communication through time, feedback, and interaction.

Topics

  • motion principles
  • transitions
  • animation
  • state changes
  • affordances
  • feedback systems
  • time-based communication

Project Direction

Design a motion-enhanced interactive experience.

Unit 4 — Responsive and Generative Systems

Focus

Design systems that adapt to context, content, and emerging technologies.

Topics

  • responsive systems
  • dynamic content
  • APIs
  • computational design
  • AI-assisted workflows
  • generative processes
  • adaptive experiences

Project Direction

Develop a responsive, generative, or adaptive design system.

Cross-Sequence Themes

The following themes are introduced, revisited, and expanded throughout both semesters.

Theme DT I DT II

Creative Confidence Exploration and experimentation Prototyping and iteration
Systems Thinking Organization and structure Components and scalability
Professional Practice Workflow and production Collaboration and documentation
Emerging Technology Contemporary tools and AI literacy Generative and adaptive systems
Technology as Systems Materials, media, and standards Platforms, components, and behavior

These themes function as recurring lenses rather than isolated topics.

Sophomore Outcome

Upon completion of the sequence, students will be able to:

  • work fluently across print, screen, and web environments
  • understand material and digital production systems
  • organize and manage complex design assets
  • translate information across media
  • understand how technologies shape communication
  • create structured design systems
  • distinguish between assets and reusable components
  • prototype and evaluate interactive experiences
  • design responsive and scalable solutions
  • collaborate within professional workflows
  • critically engage emerging technologies
  • apply accessibility, ethics, and advocacy principles to design practice

The sequence establishes a foundation for advanced study while preparing students for a rapidly evolving technological landscape.
