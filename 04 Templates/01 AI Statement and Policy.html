Boilerplate Artificial Intelligence (AI) Syllabus Statement

Semester Overview

The semester will be organized around three major projects, each requiring five weeks to complete. Projects will include regular exercises or checkpoints where work will be due; these are to help students progress and develop work toward final reviews. Learning objectives, core concepts, and overall project briefs are shared across studios. Each studio, however, will make very different work because of the diversity of creative perspectives across the faculty and student body. Full project descriptions, rubrics, and schedules are provided by each instructor, typically via Canvas modules.

AI Use in Graphic Design Studios

The role of artificial intelligence (AI) tools in graphic design studios varies by course, depending on learning objectives, course content, and instructor expectations. Some studios may incorporate AI-assisted tools for ideation, image generation, research, or workflow development, while others may limit or prohibit AI use to emphasize foundational skills, creative process, and critical thinking. 

Although learning objectives, core concepts, and overall project briefs are shared across studio sections, each studio will produce distinct outcomes that reflect the diverse creative perspectives of both faculty and students. Detailed project descriptions, grading rubrics, schedules, and AI-use expectations are provided by individual instructors, typically through Canvas modules.

Boilerplate Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy Statement

Because graphic design spans everything from conceptual ideation to technical execution, this boilerplate is designed to be modular. It uses a "traffic light" framing so you can easily adjust the boundaries based on the specific goals of your course.

Department of Graphic Design: Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy

Statement of Principle

In the Department of Graphic Design at Iowa State University, we view emerging technologies as powerful tools that can expand, but never replace, human creativity, critical thinking, and ethical responsibility. Graphic design is fundamentally about human communication, empathy, and intent. While Generative AI (such as ChatGPT, Midjourney, DALL-E, and Adobe Firefly) can assist in the creative workflow, your value as a designer lies in your unique voice, your iterative process, and your ability to synthesize complex ideas.

🟢 Option 1: Broad Integration (Green Light)

  • Policy: Generative AI tools are permitted and encouraged in this course as a supplement to your creative process. You may use them for brainstorming, mood-boarding, conceptual ideation, or asset generation.
  • Condition: AI must be treated as a collaborative assistant, not the sole creator. You must document your use of AI (prompts used, platforms utilized) and disclose it in your process books/project presentations. All final compositions must reflect your own significant design intervention, layout, and typographic choices.

🟡 Option 2: Guided/Conditional Use (Yellow Light)

  • Policy: AI tools may be used only for specific assignments or phases of a project as explicitly outlined by the instructor (e.g., using ChatGPT to generate copy for a packaging layout, or Firefly to expand a background image).
  • Condition: Unauthorized use of AI for core creative deliverables (such as generating final logos, complete layouts, or writing design rationale essays) will be considered a violation of academic integrity. When allowed, its use must be explicitly cited.

🔴 Option 3: Traditional/Foundational Focus (Red Light)

  • Policy: The use of generative AI tools is not permitted in this course.
  • Rationale: This course focuses on building foundational skills—such as formal composition, manual typography, grid structures, and technical software mastery—that require hands-on, human execution. Submitting AI-generated imagery, code, or layouts for any assignment will be treated as academic misconduct.

Expectations, Ethics, and Academic Integrity

Regardless of the tier selected above, the following universal rules apply to all graphic design coursework:

  • Ownership and Plagiarism: Presenting AI-generated work as your own completely unaided creation is a violation of the ISU Academic Misconduct policy. If an AI tool is used to generate an asset, that asset must be attributed.
  • The "Copyright" Problem: Current legal frameworks generally dictate that AI-generated imagery cannot be copyrighted, and many AI datasets scrape artists' work without consent. As professional designers in training, you must be aware of the ethical implications of using these tools for commercial client work.
  • Accuracy and Bias: AI tools frequently output "hallucinations" (false facts) and visual biases. If you use AI for research or copywriting, you are entirely responsible for fact-checking and refining the output.
  • Grading Criteria: You are graded on your design process (sketching, iteration, critique, and refinement), not just the final artifact. An AI-generated output that lacks a documented human process will not meet the learning objectives of this department.

University Compliance: Any unauthorized use of AI that violates this syllabus will be reported to the Office of Student Conduct in accordance with Iowa State University’s Academic Integrity regulations.
